## Contradictions / open ranges
- C-1 Ice-shell thickness: S6 (NASA Enceladus) gives 1–5 km at the south pole / 20–25 km global average; the NASA Ocean Worlds infographic (S1) gives 20–25 miles (30–40 km) at Enceladus' south pole; S9 (AGU) gives 20–40 km with the south pole as small as 5 km. These differ — treat as an unresolved range, not a single value.
- C-2 Europa ice shell: S3 (NASA) says 15–25 km; S4/S5 (Juno/Nature Astronomy) give ~29 km regionally (pure-water conductive layer); pre-Juno literature spans 3–45 km (S5). No single agreed thickness.
- C-3 Enceladus ocean depth: S1/S6 describe a global ocean but give no single depth; NASA EMF (S7) cites ~65 km in one estimate. Unresolved.

- No claim is asserted from memory; any fact not traceable to S1–S9 is omitted.
