## Chapter 1 — The crew and their ships (~2 min)
Who flew and what they flew: McDivitt (CDR), Scott (CMP), Schweickart (LMP); Gumdrop (CSM-104) and Spider (LM-3); Saturn V SA-504; launch March 3 1969; heaviest crewed spacecraft to Earth orbit to that time (95,231 lb).

Claim IDs: A9-005, A9-006, A9-007, A9-009, A9-010, A9-021.

## Chapter 2 — First docking: transposition and docking (~2 min)
CSM separates from S-IVB, turns around, docks with Spider — first successful docking of the Apollo program; springs eject the docked pair from the S-IVB; S-IVB restarted twice to simulate TLI and sent to solar orbit.

Claim IDs: A9-017, A9-018, A9-037.  
Graphic: animated timeline of transposition & docking (separation → turn → dock → eject), with MET timestamps.  
Footage: docking sequence archival; S-IVB separation stills.

## Chapter 3 — Spider's systems check (~2 min)
Flight day 3: McDivitt and Schweickart spend 8 hours in Spider; multiple descent-engine firings testing automatic and manual thrust control; landing and rendezvous radar tests; 5-minute TV broadcast from inside Spider; first docked SPS burn.

Claim IDs: A9-011, A9-012, A9-013, A9-014, A9-015, A9-037.  
Graphic: numbers card (8 hours in LM, N descent-engine firings, N radar tests) + thrust-control schematic (auto vs manual).

## Chapter 4 — Schweickart's EVA: the PLSS test (~2 min)
Planned 2-hour EVA scrubbed/rescheduled to 37.5 minutes after Schweickart's space motion sickness; Schweickart on Spider's front porch testing the PLSS backpack (first/only pre-Moon-landing EVA; only chance to test the backpack in space); Scott stand-up EVA from Gumdrop hatch filming him; 25-foot nylon rope Schweickart's only tether; handrails found easy.

Claim IDs: A9-025, A9-026, A9-027, A9-028, A9-029, A9-030, A9-031.  
Graphic: EVA timeline (illness → scrub → 37.5-min porch EVA) + PLSS backpack parts card (oxygen, cooling water, fan).  
Footage: Schweickart on the porch archival; Scott in Gumdrop hatch filming; PLSS/still suit stills.

## Chapter 5 — Spider flies free: LM free flight (~3 min)
Flight day 5 principal objective: undock Spider, test descent engine again, jettison descent stage, fire ascent engine simulating lunar-orbit rendezvous; separation maneuvers (DPS 19 s at 10% then 40% to 50 miles; 22 s to 100 miles); rendezvous (RCS 32 s jettison descent stage; APS 3 s + 38 s intercept; course corrections; stop 100 feet; pitchover; Scott's "biggest, friendliest, funniest looking Spider"; smooth docking); flew separately 6h23m; ascent stage fired to fuel depletion into elliptical orbit (decayed Oct 23 1981); descent stage decayed March 22.

Claim IDs: A9-020, A9-021, A9-022, A9-023, A9-024, A9-032.  
Graphic: separation/rendezvous map (50 miles → 100 miles → 100 feet → dock) with burn times; ascent-stage orbit-decay card.  
Footage: Spider free-flight archival; ascent-stage separation stills; Gumdrop-as-seen-from-Spider stills.

## Chapter 6 — The S-IVB near-miss (~1.5 min)
An S-IVB restart produced less than the desired velocity increase, off nominal by about 11 percent; it did not affect Apollo 9 but a lunar mission might have been aborted — the first hint that a single engine shortfall could end a Moon landing.

Claim IDs: A9-037.  
Graphic: numbers card (11 percent off nominal) + S-IVB restart schematic.  
Footage: S-IVB stage stills; engine-test stills.

## Chapter 7 — Home: reentry and splashdown (~1.5 min)
Eighth/final SPS burn (just under 12 s) brings Apollo 9 out of orbit; heat shield max 2,700°F while crew stayed at room temperature; ionized-gas 4-minute radio blackout; drogue then three mains at 10,000 ft (~22 mph); splashdown 670 miles south-southwest of Bermuda, 3 miles from USS Guadalcanal, within 3 miles and in full view; 241 h 54 s (10 s over plan); 151 revolutions; crew aboard 49 minutes after splashdown; 350-pound cake.

Claim IDs: A9-033, A9-034, A9-035, A9-036, A9-039, A9-041, A9-042.  
Graphic: reentry profile card (2,700°F heat shield / room-temp cabin / 4-min blackout) + splashdown location pin.  
Footage: reentry/splashdown archival; recovery-ship and cake stills.

## Chapter 8 — Why it mattered (~1 min)
Apollo 9 qualified the LM for lunar orbit: first manned flight of all lunar hardware in Earth orbit, first manned Apollo docking, first docked SPS burn, first manned Apollo undocking, first manned LM-to-CSM docking; everything Apollo 10 and 11 needed had been proven in Earth orbit. Schweickart's PLSS became the backpack used on the Moon; Scott's stand-up EVA techniques carried over.

Claim IDs: A9-002, A9-010, A9-019, A9-031.  
Graphic: "Apollo 9 → Apollo 11" bridge card (what Apollo 9 proved → what Apollo 11 used).  
Footage: Apollo 11 launch/landing stills (for contrast, not Apollo 9 footage) — describe only.
