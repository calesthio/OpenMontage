# Speculative Evolution Pilot Script — "How could life survive beneath a frozen alien ocean?"

**Topic:** How could life survive beneath a frozen alien ocean?
**Target length:** 10–12 minutes (narration).
**Status:** DRAFT, NOT_PUBLISHABLE.
**Method:** every sentence carries `claim_ids` resolving to docs/openmontage/evidence/S1_SPECULATIVE_CLAIM_LEDGER.md; real-science claims are KNOWN (TWO sources), invented creature designs are SPECULATION with a justifying constraint; no facts from memory.

## 3 truthful titles

1. How Could Life Survive Beneath a Frozen Alien Ocean?
2. Europa and Enceladus: Oceans Beneath the Ice
3. Chemosynthesis: Life Without Sunlight

## Hook

- Beneath miles of ice, on a frozen moon, there could be an ocean. [SE-01]
- On Jupiter's moon Europa, a global saltwater sea lies hidden under a completely frozen crust. [SE-01, SE-04]
- That ocean holds more than twice as much water as all of Earth's oceans combined. [SE-02]
- And it is kept liquid not by sunlight, but by tidal heating from Jupiter. [SE-03]
- If Earth's deep ocean can support life without sunlight, could a frozen alien ocean do the same? [SE-08]

## Chapter 1 — The Earth analogue: deep-sea vents

- On Earth, the answer is already written in the dark. [SE-08]
- Miles below the surface, where sunlight never reaches, hydrothermal vents spew mineral-rich water into the freezing deep. [SE-08]
- There, life does not run on photosynthesis. It runs on chemosynthesis. [SE-08]
- Chemosynthetic bacteria harvest chemical energy from hydrogen sulfide and carbon dioxide, turning inorganic chemicals into food. [SE-08]
- Those bacteria form the base of an entire food web — vent shrimp, tubeworms, mussels — all living without a single photon. [SE-08]
- This is our template: water, chemistry, and an energy source, all independent of the sun. [SE-08]

## Chapter 2 — Europa: an ocean beneath the ice

- Europa is about the size of Earth's Moon, but its secrets are below the surface. [SE-01]
- Its ice crust is tens of miles thick, and the ocean beneath is completely frozen at the surface. [SE-04]
- Yet below that ice, the ocean holds more than twice as much water as all of Earth's oceans. [SE-02]
- Jupiter's gravity flexes the moon as it orbits, creating tidal heating that keeps the water liquid. [SE-03]
- That same flexing drives chemistry between the ocean and a rocky sea floor. [SE-03]
- Water, chemistry, energy: Europa may have nearly all the ingredients for a habitable world. [SE-01, SE-02, SE-03]

## Chapter 3 — Enceladus: plumes and hydrothermal vents

- Saturn's moon Enceladus goes further: it sprays its ocean into space. [SE-05]
- Cassini flew through plumes of ice and vapor erupting from cracks at the south pole. [SE-05]
- The grains in those plumes formed where liquid water met hot rock above 90 degrees Celsius — evidence of hydrothermal vents on the ocean floor. [SE-06]
- Cassini also detected molecular hydrogen in the plume, a chemical energy source that microbes could pair with dissolved carbon dioxide. [SE-07]
- Enceladus may have nearly all the ingredients for habitability, vented straight into space. [SE-05, SE-06, SE-07]

## Chapter 4 — From measurement to invention

- So a frozen alien ocean can have water, chemistry, and an energy source. [SE-03, SE-07]
- What it needs next is a food web — and that is where we move from measurement to invention. [SE-08]
- Every creature in this episode is an invented design choice, honestly marked speculation, each justified by a physical constraint. [SE-09, SE-10, SE-11, SE-12, SE-13]
- None of them have been observed. They are plausible, constrained, and unproven. [SE-09, SE-10, SE-11, SE-12, SE-13]

## Chapter 5 — The vent grazer

- Meet the vent grazer: six limbs built to grip vent chimneys under crushing pressure. [SE-09]
- Its energy source is chemosynthetic bacteria; it grazes the bacterial mats that coat the chimneys. [SE-09]
- Six limbs give it a stable stance on a chimney swaying in the vent flow. [SE-09]
- The high pressure of the deep ocean shapes its low, broad body. [SE-09]

## Chapter 6 — The filter drifter and the ice burrower

- The filter drifter swims with an undulating fin, sieving suspended chemosynthetic microbes from neutrally buoyant water. [SE-10]
- Its energy source is suspended microbes; its locomotion is an undulating fin. [SE-10]
- The ice burrower moves by peristaltic burrowing through near-freezing, high-pressure ocean-floor sediment. [SE-11]
- Its constraint is temperature and pressure: insulated for the cold deep, built to push through sediment under load. [SE-11]

## Chapter 7 — Reproduction and bioluminescence

- In the cold dark, the vent grazer reproduces by budding at the vents, where chemical energy is most abundant. [SE-12]
- Its energy source is chemical; its reproduction is budding, timed to the richest chemical flux. [SE-12]
- It signals with bioluminescence — light made for a world that never sees the sun. [SE-13]
- In the permanently dark ocean, light is the only language that travels. [SE-13]

## Chapter 8 — How we would know

- We do not yet know if any of these creatures exist. [SE-09, SE-10, SE-11, SE-12, SE-13]
- Europa Clipper will measure the ice shell, the ocean, and the surface chemistry. [SE-01, SE-03]
- Future missions could sample Enceladus' plumes directly, looking for the chemistry of life. [SE-05, SE-07]
- Until then, the vent grazer, the filter drifter, and the ice burrower remain speculation — plausible, constrained, and unproven. [SE-09, SE-10, SE-11, SE-12, SE-13]

## Chapter 9 — Closing

- A frozen alien ocean may be the best place in the solar system to look for life. [SE-01, SE-03]
- Not because we have found it, but because the physics allows it. [SE-03, SE-07]
- Water, chemistry, energy: the ingredients are there. [SE-03]
- The rest is a question for the next mission. [SE-01, SE-03]
