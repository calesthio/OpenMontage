# Sunland Dental Care — Cosmetic (atelier)

## Design read
**Light through porcelain.** Enamel and porcelain are translucent; a veneer is half a
millimetre of glass-like ceramic. The whole piece is built on translucency, layered light and
negative space — and on *subtraction*, because the practice's actual position is that most
people asking for veneers don't need them. Nothing in this film shouts. A loud cosmetic ad
would contradict its own argument.

## Why not the obvious direction
The obvious cosmetic-dentistry film is before/after smiles, sparkle transitions and a
whitening gradient. We can't shoot before/afters (no consented cases) and shouldn't fake them.
Good — that direction is also everyone else's. Porcelain-as-material is specific to this
subject and to this dentist's conservatism.

## Dials
- `visual_variance`: high — every beat changes primary subject, scale and register
- `motion_intensity`: low — slow eases, light sweeps, everything settles; no bounce, no whip
- `information_density`: low — one idea per beat, large type, long holds

## Type
Poppins Bold for statements, Poppins Light for support — carried from the brand, but driven to
a far wider scale contrast than the implant film (120px statements against 30px support).
Bundled locally; no webfont fetch at render time.

## Signature device — used once
**A porcelain shell descending onto a tooth and catching the light.** It is the literal
definition of a veneer, and it belongs to beat 4 alone. It does not scaffold the other scenes.

## Scene plan — primary subject must change every beat
| # | Beat | Primary subject | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The hook | Type, on near-black | Intimate, still, high contrast |
| 2 | Often it's less | Three porcelain shells as objects | Airy, wide, light |
| 3 | Since 1991 | A measured line — time | Graphic, calm, horizontal |
| 4 | What a veneer is | **The signature device** | Close, slow, reverent |
| 5 | The menu | Three distinct marks | Rhythmic, even |
| 6 | End card | The brand mark | Still |

## Distinctness check
Could this be any other practice's film? No — it argues *against* the sale, which only works
for a dentist whose documented habit is talking patients out of treatment. Does it reuse a look
I've built before? No: the implant film was chips, checklists and a rotating implant on cream.
This is translucency, darkness in the opening, and a single material as the hero.
